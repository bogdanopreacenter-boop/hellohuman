Bookshops and cafés

The same faces,
every week.

One table, one hour, one subject announced in advance. Your staff hosts it. People come for the subject, stay for the room, and by the fourth week they are yours.

Start a series
No timers, no roundsNobody is moved anywhere. It is a table with a subject and someone who keeps it going.
The subject gets deeperWeek one is light on purpose. By the fourth edition the room can hold a conversation the first one could not.
Publishers pay for thisForty people discussing a book for an hour beats a launch where thirty listen and leave.

Passengers tell us when they board. We only seat people whose waiting overlaps, and the session closes before the earliest departure. Nobody misses anything, and nobody has to invent an excuse to leave.

Why it works

People like you more
than you think.

After a conversation, people systematically underestimate how much the other person liked them and enjoyed their company. The effect lasts for months.

Boothby, Cooney, Sandstrom & Clark, Psychological Science, 2018

Commuters asked to talk to a stranger enjoyed the journey more than those left alone — the opposite of what they had predicted.

Epley & Schroeder, Journal of Experimental Psychology, 2014

Almost nobody acts on this, because the prediction is made before the conversation and the correction only arrives after. That gap is the whole reason this exists.
